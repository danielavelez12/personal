The way I envision the world progressing is like water making its way down a mountain. There’s a natural sense of gravity pulling the water through, but it will branch into streams, each one finding the path of least resistance. Some patches will remain dry because of physical barriers, like a large set of rocks in the way. Suddenly, pressure builds and a tree falls, resulting in a removal of the dam, and the water will flow down an untouched patch. The river paths are determined by the volume supplied by the rainfall, the geographical landscape, and unexpected shocks to the system.

The rainfall fueling technological development is capital. Our water cycle in the next decade will grow in volume, due to growing optimism. Currently, the U.S. is the beacon of global optimism - with a relatively strong middle class, attractive cities for the wealthy, powerful innovation talent pipeline, etc., it’s receiving increasing investment from firms abroad like Softbank and overall countries like Japan.

According to Ray Dalio, for as long as the U.S. avoids the combination of 1) internal conflict, 2) external conflict, and 3) market downturn, it will continue to prosper. If there’s a global shift of power to a rising developing country, like China, India, or Brazil, funding will then flow to that country instead, but regardless will continue to grow. The current young adult generation, Gen-Z, only knows of world war through our aging grandparents and sees the world as leagues away from the recession of 2008.

To avoid a funding bubble, countries are racing to develop stronger supply to match the capital flow, similar to how Japan solved its inflation issues in the early 2000s. Company valuations will pan out as long as companies have the supply chain necessary to continue delivering value, and given a stable economy, there will be demand to pay. Therefore, we maintain worldwide trust that a dollar invested can yield 10 dollars in value.

Where will the rain flow? People will always pay for entertainment, beauty, love, health, safety, and comfort, and these are the forces of gravity that are in play. I think in the next 10 years, we’ll move boulders and unlock waterfalls like:

- 68 -> 95% 5+ year survival rate for people diagnosed with cancer. Cancer will become a tractable illness.
- Human evolution via gene editing. The wealthy will engineer their children to be infection-resistant, mutation-free, intelligent, and good looking.
- Cyborg-like desk job work. When working, we’ll have BCI combined with powerful AI-first software to transfer the context and/or signals from our thinking into plans and actions.
- Supersonic planes. People and goods will arrive to their destinations two times faster.

Changes will always happen in a chain, driven by previous technological breakthroughs and pushed forth by continuous improvements. Technological improvement is optimizing for an ideal human being, fully efficient in all of our tasks and endeavors. Like with chips and compute, infrastructure breakthroughs will help open up rivers of change in the next 10 years.
