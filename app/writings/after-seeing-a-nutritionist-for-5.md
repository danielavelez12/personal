In February of this year, I met Renee, my wonderful nutritionist who dials in from North Carolina every month to check in on me. I figured if there’s any defaults I fix in my 20s, it should be my food defaults. Almost half a year in, I’ve started to put my diet on autopilot, and the best thing is I feel confident I’m powering my mental wellbeing, strengthening my body, and extending my life, without sacrificing yummy food.

I used to be afraid of food. As a little girl, I had a list of about 5 foods I would tolerate, and no number of doctor or psychologist appointments would convince me otherwise. (I was fun to bring to family gatherings.) When I went to college and started cooking, that’s when things changed. I discovered a world of flavors; strawberries, greens, potatoes, sushi, etc. I still remember my first tangerine. Now, I ask my nutritionist every month what new dishes or recipes I should try - I have so many options that I need help picking.

My first appointment with Renee, I was a bit nervous. I thankfully don’t have any eating disorders or gut health problems, so I wasn’t sure what we were going to talk about. But we seamlessly launched into a one-hour conversation about everything I wanted to improve about myself: my energy levels, muscle strength, mental focus, acne, sleep quality, etc.

Here’s a few things I’ve learned through our meetings:

**If you’re hungry, just eat more.**

One of the problems I came to Renee with was, I honestly just gethungry sometimes and don’t know what to eat. 2 hours after a fast-casual bowl lunch, I’d be scavenging for snacks. Even more, I was always feeling guilty for not trying intermittent fasting and not being more “antifragile” like the tech bro books had told me to be. Renee pushed me to ignore the fasting if it doesn’t work for me, and just… eat more. My breakfast is sacred to me now - 4 eggs with veggies, oatmeal, and a slice of bread. When I order my lunch meals, I add in an extra serving of protein or veggies, and later in the afternoon I’ll munch on some carbohydrates (e.g. fruits) and more protein / fats (greek yogurt or nuts).

**Feed the brain.**

Based on research from 2023, the [Mediterranean-DASH Intervention for Neurodegenerative Delay](https://www.nejm.org/doi/full/10.1056/NEJMoa2302368) (MIND) diet reduces brain inflammation and “oxidative stress,” or an overload of unstable molecules that damage the brain. Even though it primarily helps mitigate age-related cognitive decline, it also enhances mental speed in middle-aged adults. My top brain foods are:

- Spinach or collard greens in my eggs
- Broccoli added into lunch orders
- Salmon bowls
- Whole-wheat bread and oats in the morning
- Chia seeds in my oatmeal
- Sources of unsaturated fat like olive oil

A lot of these ingredients have unsaturated fats, or “healthy fats,” which are healthier than saturated fats because of their chemical structure. They contain one or more double bond, which means they can’t pack as tightly and are more fluid. Instead of causing inflammation like saturated fats do, they actually help the body, ensuring molecules are highly functional with flexible membranes and helping produce anti-inflammatory molecules.

**Get your vitamins in (but only however much you need).**

Vitamins power our bodily functions, and we can get enough of them through a varied diet. Vitamin D, which helps keep you healthy and mentally sharp, is in fatty fish (e.g. salmon) and fortified yogurt or milk. For us New Yorkers, we can take supplements during the winter, but otherwise supplements aren’t necessary at all (and overdosing can be pretty bad). Supplements are also not as strictly regulated - they can be sold without prior FDA approval, so it’s important to go with trusted brands (e.g. Nature Made).

**Overall health is key.**

Renee has honestly served as my therapist, life coach, and nutritionist all-in-one. She helps keep me accountable to my fitness, skincare, and sleep goals and my goal of making it to the grocery store at least once a week. From mid-May to mid-June, I got sick three times, which was due to lots of things unrelated to nutrition. Every month Renee and I will evaluate what’s working and what’s not working and iterate - when to schedule workouts or friend hangouts? How to get myself to bed earlier? How to stay sane while working at an early-stage startup? The answers to all my life questions usually consist of balance and self care, and sometimes, if I’m lucky, a bit of dark chocolate.
