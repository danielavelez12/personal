Yesterday we lost a close family friend to brain cancer. For a year, he fought hard, writing about his battle and even returning excitedly to his executive role at Google while still undergoing electric fields therapy. Ultimately, the cancer resurfaced and there was nothing more the doctors could do.

He was the dad of my childhood best friend who I grew up with, both of us little girls in Atlanta frolicking in the autumn leaves and racing our bikes around the quiet neighborhood. My memories of him are wisps of warmth and laughter, fleeting but tender. As a curious 6 year old, I took in everything about the way he treated my friend, her sister, and her mom. The way he would make my friend’s mom chuckle with soft-spoken jokes, or how he would slyly start up father-daughter tickle wars, or how he would parent with a stern yet thoughtful energy. I remember him in the front seat driving us on roadtrips or at the backyard grill cooking us a meal.

Whenever we’ve gone back to visit them in Atlanta, I’ve been transported back to my child self, feeling the warmth and safety of being near a secondary father figure. One time we took refuge in their home during a hurricane and played board games over candlelight when the electricity went out. What stands out is how I’ve always felt his care, despite no longer being the little girls we were.

When my parents called with the news, I’d never felt more frustrated by my limited Spanish. I didn’t have the words to express what I wanted to say, and even without the language barrier, it still felt hard to find them. Reaching out to my friend was even harder.

Apart from grieving, I’ve been reflecting on how things and people can come back into our lives unexpectedly, many years later. Yesterday I scrolled through my emails with my friend, from back when we were 9 years old and nurturing our long-distance friendship after my move to Florida. I was 1) a know-it-all and 2) terribly missing my friend.

How could a S.B.B.F.T.N.E.C.B become someone I haven’t seen in years? As an adult, I’ve tried to mentally let go of friendships that I’ve failed to maintain, being in opposite parts of the country and in completely different mental spaces. But it’s been hard. When I read Ava’s “[do we ever get over things](https://www.avabear.xyz/p/do-we-ever-get-over-things),” I felt so understood - maybe a piece of us always remains attached to each relationship we build throughout our lives. I still cry about missing my childhood dog, I love reunions with middle school friends, and I randomly yearn to check in on people I haven’t seen in years.

Now, my childhood best friend feels closer to me than ever. As I grieve her dad’s passing, my heart aches for her - I’ve never felt so much secondhand pain. Despite it being almost 20 years since we were growing up together, I’m reliving all those memories of her with her dad from my eyes. Up on his shoulders, or hand in his, or running to hug him after a school day.

I especially cherish friendships that have grown tender with time, and my friendship with my childhood friend feels so dear to me. I’m angry at myself for waiting until a life shock to give her a visit, but I’m hoping it’s not too late. Time feels circular to me now, and I know hugging her soon will feel like I’m right back in 2007.
