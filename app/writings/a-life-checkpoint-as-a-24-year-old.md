Today I was talking with an intern at our company who’s an MIT freshman. It was the first time I felt old. He explained the new course titles to me as I reminisced about their old names, and as he listed his classes and clubs, I racked my brain for anyone we might know in common but came at a loss. I’m now almost two years from my graduation in spring ‘23, which basically means I can no longer parade around as a new grad. I also have fully entered typical adulthood - I no longer crave going out and living “for the plot,” I love my sleep, and I’ll be renewing my teenage passport soon. Adulthood kind of came out of nowhere, to be honest.

So, what has my time out of college looked like so far? In general, it’s been a lot of ups and downs. No consistent success trajectory yet, but definitely constant learning.

### **2023 Summer - 2024 Summer**

The previous year, I built myself up and gained confidence.

**I took some risks, faced my fears, and made big decisions.**

I skydived, played my first senior piano recital, shipped lots of code as an early-stage engineer, made close friends in NYC, and traveled for two months to Colombia to dedicate time to family. Life was a thrill, I loved finally having my hands on the wheel and being able to decide the direction of my life.

**By the end of this year, I felt like I could commit to things.**

Throughout the year, I looked back on the things I had successfully committed to, such as a long-term relationship, a consistent workout schedule, and a good credit score. My meal prep and skincare routine habits fully formed. This all gave me conviction that I could take on endeavors and see them through.

**I set simple goals and executed on them.**

I started up a variety of hobbies in NYC, such as event-planning, hiking, playing in an orchestra, and writing. During 2024 spring, I co-hosted a hackathon, piano concert, and monthly group hikes upstate. I ran my first half-marathon in under 2 hours. My hobby goals were very reasonable - I was just building the muscle of *doing* things, with no need to reach perfection, and relatively low stakes. At work, I had higher stakes, but I felt like I was setting measured goals and hitting them, successfully meeting team expectations.

### **2024 Summer - 2024 EOY**

I then bundled up my confidence and tried harder things. It was tough, to say the least.

**I put myself out there, and fell short.**

I moved up next to the first chair of my violin section in orchestra and played a piano solo at Merkin Hall in Manhattan. Hundreds of people in NYC paid money to listen to my playing up close and personal. Was my playing worthy of the spotlight? Unclear - my first chair sternly reminded me in an email that I’d have to re-audition next orchestra season, and I didn’t receive any invitations to play piano again following my solo performance. I set the bar high for myself but failed to reach it.

**I took risks with high stakes, and reached bottom.**

At the beginning of the summer, Alza closed down. This was the first blow of this period — I lost the personal savings I had invested by exercising my shares, and I had to see our company, everything we had built over the year, being torn down. Shortly after, I flew to SF for new opportunities and ended up deciding to start a company of my own. For 4 months, my co-founder and I built software for a few banks that we got in touch with, thanks to some warm introductions and cold email crafting. We were building our dream world of streamlined financial services. As soon as we were ready to take in angel funding and sign a paid contract, though, I realized we weren’t a co-founder fit. I tried mustering up the energy to pivot and continue as a solo founder, but I had no money left, or confidence. I had to reach out to all the people that had believed and invested in me, explaining that I was taking a step away from the startup, even though it had only been a few months.

**I put a lot on my plate, and I dropped the ball.**

Working on my own startup gave me gray hairs, sleep issues, and painful acne problems. I was in such a frazzled state that I became a careless, aloof friend, with multiple friends bringing up their concerns to me. I interviewed and hastily approved a new roommate, and ended up inviting a troubled alcoholic roommate into our home. We had to live with her for more than a month before we were able to move her out.

**I gave myself no breaks, and my habits fell apart.**

Days after deciding to part ways with my co-founder, I rushed straight into my new role at another startup. I was simultaneously winding down my startup and ramping up with my new engineering team. While in Colombia with my family, I worked tirelessly on holidays, at airports and in cars, and late nights, in order to keep up with co-workers and make an impact as a new hire. My habits fell apart. I hemorrhaged money due to messy finances and un-cancelled B2B subscriptions, and my credit score plummeted. By the end of 2024, I had lost my fitness routine and all my habits I had built earlier that year, such as journaling, writing, or reading.

**I pushed myself past my limits.**

I decided to go on a 7-day backpacking trip in Patagonia, without training beforehand. We summited mountain and hiked over 80 km. I had to take Advil in order to sleep well at night through the knee ache, and the last day I was hobbling with intense joint pain. I thankfully didn’t get injured, but I could feel myself getting closer to it than I’ve ever been.

### **Where I am now**

Despite the hardships of the past half-year, I’m glad I attempted hard things with relatively high-stakes, because I never would have known what I was capable of otherwise. My world has been opened and everything feels in reach, and I know what I need to do to get places. My self-confidence still took a hit, but I feel 10 years wiser. I’m more empathetic and thoughtful about the decisions I make, conscious of my limits and the potential risks. My discipline is stronger because I have a better sense of the future implications of my work and habits.

Now begins the recovery, rebuilding, and improvement phase. I’m carving out time to recover my financial health and restore my friendships, and planning my life around the habits I want to re-establish. I’ve got larger future goals in mind - another startup, a bigger piano performance, and more, and I now understand the importance of taking care of myself on the way.

A couple months into my new startup role, I received some shoutouts during our all-hands for the projects I’ve shipped, and I’m tackling harder challenges involving more uncertainty and larger business outcomes. I’m back to writing and I’ve started going to a nearby climbing gym. Coming up, a dermatologist appointment and a weekend trip with my close friends.

One thing about adulthood I’ve learned (now that I can preach about being old) is that we have more time than we think. We can either spend it poorly - on bad habits or buried in one thing - or well. The results can be drastically different. In order to spend our time well, we have to have good drivers and clear values. With so much more clarity now, I feel excited about what the next six months, and years, will bring.
