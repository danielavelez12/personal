The past year at the Brooklyn Symphony Orchestra, we’ve been in trials with potential new conductors - our previous one led the orchestra for twenty years (!). At the start of the process, hundreds of conductors applied, and the orchestra exec committee chose 5 finalists to take turns being our conductor for a concert cycle.

A couple of our auditioning conductors:

- David, who makes at least 5 Star Trek references per rehearsal and gets extremely excited about playing [Copland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Copland) music (fun fact, he went to Copland’s bar mitzvah)

Conducting our orchestra (or any orchestra) isn’t easy. The orchestra sprawls across four string sections, horns, percussion, winds, piano or harp, and more, that all need to play in sync. We only have ~6 rehearsals to put together a concert of 2+ hours of music. Whoever will be our conductor will also be the “artistic director” in general, which means they make all decisions on behalf of the orchestra (music, venues, strategy, etc.)

Here’s some things I thought made each conductor great:

1. **They understand the orchestra.** Felipe knows exactly how to work with us - the first few rehearsals, he has us play the music excruciatingly slow so that we can iron out the notes and focus on precision first. This motivates us to practice more at home, earlier on. He chooses a repertoire that isn’t overly challenging yet showcases us in the right ways. During rehearsals, he knows exactly what parts to drill - the tricky entrances, complex divisis, weird syncopations, blurry melody lines, etc.
2. **They’re thoughtful, prepared, and opinionated.** There’s so much left to interpretation in music, and it’s up to the conductor to choose how to say each phrase, voice each line, and bring out the details of each piece. David sends us email essays (hundreds of lines) after rehearsals with his notes on how we should think about playing each measure of each piece. E.g. “Please use a very soft sound throughout this section… this must float.” or “The F-natural on this downbeat is extremely important.” or “The sound should never let go, continually moving forward.” He comes in with a fully formed vision of what the piece should sound like, and this guides his coaching.
3. **They know how to communicate their vision.** Knowing the right sound is one thing, and communicating it to us is another. Both David and Felipe have great mannerisms around this, whether it’s singing or scatting, using facial expressions and gestures, or coming up with metaphors. Felipe will say the tempo is “non-negotiable” when we should strictly play in time, and David will tell the violins to stop “screaming” when we’re playing too harshly. Felipe contorts his face and pretends to knead dough in order to visually embody what our playing should sound like.
4. **They’re entrepreneurial.** It’s not easy to stand out as an orchestra in NYC, and both David and Felipe have helped us make face. David prepared a slideshow for our concert and spoke about how each piece tied into the concert theme, even paying homage to Brooklyn, NY, with the Copland. Felipe is a master networker and has gotten crazy gigs, and has brought the orchestra along with his career growth so far.
5. **They conduct like no one is watching (or like everyone is).** When David and Felipe conduct, they are fully immersed and performing with their whole body and soul. Whenever there’s a dramatic entrance, David will stomp and point with the baton like Harry Potter casting an expecto patronum spell. Felipe will furrow his brows, shake his fists, or dance in order to pull the music out of us in a certain way. It works - the orchestra falls right under their magic.

If anything, I’ve learned an orchestra’s success is significantly dependent on having the right conductor. We have extremely talented people on their own, but we only come together to play beautiful, meaningful music with a great conductor at the helm.
