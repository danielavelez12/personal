I’m writing this from our hostel room back in Puerto Natales, as I’m lying on a bed for the first time again in 7 days. My knees ache but my mind is beaming. I’ve wrapped up one of the hardest personal challenges of my life - hiking around 80-100 km across the O trek in the Torres del Paine park in Patagonia. To follow are some tales of our adventures.

**Day 0 - Fueling up (Puerto Natales)**

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!HIZH!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F66e2500d-c1dc-4faa-99e7-0ebceff6cb6d_6000x4000.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!HIZH!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F66e2500d-c1dc-4faa-99e7-0ebceff6cb6d_6000x4000.jpeg)

Prior to our journey, we checked all the boxes on every suggested packing list we could find. We rented hiking poles, sleeping bags, mugs, and a gas canister, and stocked up on freeze-dried meals and snacks. That night, we sipped on Chilean wine and shrimp soup over a last long meal.

**Day 1 - A bright start (Central campsite)**

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Eyc5!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa708888b-00fe-4e15-a1d5-81bc93c42a7c_6000x4000.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Eyc5!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fa708888b-00fe-4e15-a1d5-81bc93c42a7c_6000x4000.jpeg)

We arrived on bus at the Central campsite like little kids dropped off at school. The weather was dry with a strong sun and a cool breeze. We navigated around the waves of backpackers and tourists, and Arina and I set up our tent for the first time. The hike of the day was the Mirador Las Torres hike to see the Torres up close - Jaclyn and Sherry split off to do it during the day, while Arina and I napped through the afternoon with plans to do it through dawn. We also spent some time reading Plato’s Republic, the book we chose to bring to discuss throughout the week.

**Day 1/2 - Watching the sunrise (8 hr Mirador Las Torres hike)**

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!jkpW!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Feec5fb7e-cd1f-4f60-9a3b-9b997139c128_6000x4000.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!jkpW!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Feec5fb7e-cd1f-4f60-9a3b-9b997139c128_6000x4000.jpeg)

At 12:30 am, our alarm went off and we awoke to a chilly night. Headlamps and layers on, teeth brushed, and muscles stretched, we ventured out into the darkness. From 1 am to 5 am, we put one foot in front of the other while going up ominous mountain paths and around pitch black cliffs to reach the 900m-high summit. We finally joined a group of ~50 other hikers huddling around on the rocks in excitement for the reveal of the morning - the sun’s rays on the three towers across the water. Soon, the sun rose over the mountain, and we saw the tower tips gradually light on fire. We sat in awe for a while, before giddily heading back down.

**Day 2 - The start of the journey (4 hr to Serón campsite)**

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!4jOo!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5ad1716c-8059-41b1-9019-a952ac61879d_6000x4000.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!4jOo!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5ad1716c-8059-41b1-9019-a952ac61879d_6000x4000.jpeg)

After our sunrise hike and still no sleep, Arina and I pushed through the day and reunited with Jaclyn and Sherry. We set out to the next campsite, over hilly meadows and grassy plains. It was our first hike with our 25 lb packs, strapped tightly to our backs and buckled around our waists, requiring a few breaks along the way. The rest stops were moments of physical relief and nature appreciation. During one of our stops, we lounged with our feet up on our bags and conversed with an owl.

The Serón campsite was significantly less crowded than the Central campsite - we had entered the league of committed backpackers. The campsite was adorned with gray tents hoisted up in the trees. After setting our sweaty clothes to dry, we showered, ate our meals, and sat down on a picnic table to play poker with some cards and a notebook.

**Day 3 - Walking and more walking (6 hr to Dickson campsite)**

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!huLh!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F80fb5c40-f2ae-47a2-97c7-223e25e0ea2d_6000x4000.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!huLh!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F80fb5c40-f2ae-47a2-97c7-223e25e0ea2d_6000x4000.jpeg)

This day was long, with more winding paths and inclines than before. We passed the time by talking about each other’s families, relationships, childhood stories, political views, and life goals. The streams refilled our water bottles and the views lifted our spirits. On this route, we were turning the top right corner of the O trek, and there was something new to see on each stretch of the trail. We even crossed through a band of freely-grazing horses, waiting to be put into commission to carry materials back and forth from the park campsites.

The campsite came into view as we reached the top of a hill. We gazed at the yellow tents sprinkled around a peninsula surrounded by Lake Dickson, and rushed down to end our journey for the day. Soon after, we sat around a table at the refugio bar and devoured some freshly baked pepperoni pizza and Chilean dessert. We warmed up another set of freeze-dried meals, played another game of poker, and went to sleep early for the night.

**Day 4 - Wet, windy, and wicked cold (4 hr hike to Perros)**

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!mFCe!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd88e47b5-d9b8-46df-9fac-0533582b5740_6000x4000.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!mFCe!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd88e47b5-d9b8-46df-9fac-0533582b5740_6000x4000.jpeg)

We started out early in the morning to try to beat the colder, windier post-noon weather, but faced brutal conditions regardless. Battering rain seeped into our clothes and socks and chilled us to the bone. We kept up our pace to keep warm, splashing through rivers and scrambling up muddy forest paths. The last hour, we crossed over barren hills of rocks through torrential wind and hail, doubting our path a few times and wondering when we would ever make it to the campsite.

We finally scuttled into the warm, small refugio, packed with backpackers warming up their hands with steam and laying out their soaked clothes to dry on the kitchen tables. There was no space to sit, so we waited out the afternoon hours while standing and journaling while gazing out the window at the falling snow. At dinnertime, we met some other young folks at the table and bonded over the variety of our backpacker meals. We asked why they decided to do this trek - they said, for the challenge. Some more experienced backpackers were amused to know that this had been our choice for our first backpacking adventure.

**Day 5 - The John Gardner pass (10 hr hike to Grey)**

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!7gnb!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fad1b7fa8-2a3f-4de9-a0b2-87cf8478d7ed_6000x4000.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!7gnb!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fad1b7fa8-2a3f-4de9-a0b2-87cf8478d7ed_6000x4000.jpeg)

In the morning, the campsite ran out of paper in the bathrooms and at some point running water. We were thankful, though, because we finally had clear weather. We set out to tackle the most challenging part of the hike - the John Gardner pass. Sunglasses and hoods on and hiking poles held tight, we summited a mountain through rivers and snow-covered rocks. Through the long uphill, we discussed the meaning of justice from Plato’s perspective. At the top, we cheered and looked out at the snowy mountains glistening with broad sunlight and falling snowflakes. On the other side of the summit we could now see the Grey glacier, a giant sheet of blue ice that pushed towards the horizon. We celebrated with granola bars (though frozen) and raspberry chocolate. On the way down, each of us slipped a few times, but we weren’t fazed - we were now hiking by muscle memory. Our chatter about random topics (relationships, personal finances, or wedding ideas) continued, unfettered by the downhill challenge.

At the Grey campsite, we finally got a few hours of daylight to fully dry our clothes and sleeping bags. The forest turned into a gallery of backpacker clothing items hung on branches and flapping in the wind. This campsite had a lot more amenities than the last few, now that we were back in W-trek territory. We shared another pizza and tried some Calafate beer. At night, the four of us cozied up in a tent to watch an episode of Gossip Girl, a much needed remedy after a 10 hour hiking day.

**Day 6 - Last stretch (4 hr hike to Paine Grande)**

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Q7Ng!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F33353bb6-1495-48c1-87b4-79a60056605b_6000x4000.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!Q7Ng!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F33353bb6-1495-48c1-87b4-79a60056605b_6000x4000.jpeg)

By day 6, we had experienced all of the weather extremes of the Patagonias. We had hiked through sun, rain, and snow, and this day’s weather was extreme wind, with headwinds almost toppling us over and tailwinds violently pushing us up hills. For our one rest break, we huddled behind a bush guarding us from the wind. This last stretch of our journey also made us realize how far we had come. A 250m summit felt like a warm-up stroll.

Paine Grande was our final campsite, and we enjoyed a long conversation while sharing the last of our freeze-dried meals family-style in the refugio kitchen. We had our last tent hangout and then went to sleep on rocky ground for the last time.

**Day 7 - An attempt to complete the O trek (5 hr Frances hike)**

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!9LY1!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5755b18e-3f0c-49bf-820b-0b6bec9dfdeb_6000x4000.jpeg)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!9LY1!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5755b18e-3f0c-49bf-820b-0b6bec9dfdeb_6000x4000.jpeg)

On the last day, we started out to reach the Frances mirador, which would bring us closer to completing the whole O-trek loop. Despite our newly-found resilience and confidence after a week of hiking, we were again challenged by the Patagonian forces of nature. Relentless gusts of rain and wind pushed us in every direction as we struggled to push through our last hike. We made it to a checkpoint to view the Frances glacier perched up high in the mountains, where a snowstorm was expelling torrents of icy wind towards us.

Freezing cold, soaking, and hungry, we turned back to cut our hike a bit short. It was time to prioritize our return to a comfortable life. On the way back, we sang Broadway tunes and 2000s pop songs, a final reminder of how we were able to joyfully pass the time through any storm we were hiking through. We finished off the hike with burgers, Patagonia beers, and cards.

**End of the adventure**

This trek was harder than expected, both mentally and physically. We had no way of knowing the hourly Patagonia weather ahead of time - our bag covers and rain-jackets helped a bit, but it was mostly our morale that kept us going. (And wrapping all my things in a trash bag would’ve helped keep my sleeping bag dry.) Some of our packs didn’t fit right and I personally could’ve trained more, contributing to even more of a physical challenge. Trekking across the Torres del Paine has been a true limit test, and I’m glad we had each other and some good freeze-dried meals to carry us through.
